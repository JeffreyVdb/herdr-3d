## 2026-10-01 — #86: shadow acne from the moving sun

| # | Question | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Why vertical stripes since M17 | The sun used to stand in one direction that happened to spare the walls. With a latitude it sweeps the sky, so every wall spends the day at a grazing angle or with its back to the sun, and the shadow map striped the faces turned away from it (sofa, whiteboard, the board partition, the city's dollhouse walls): a constant depth `bias` only, no `normalBias`, and three's toon model (`ToonLightingModel`, the node path **both** backends run: `?backend=webgl2` is `WebGPURenderer` with the WebGL2 backend) steps `N·L * 0.5 + 0.5` to 0.7 / 1.0 without clamping at 0, so a back face still took 70 % of the sun **and its shadow term**. The e2e suite pins noon with no latitude (the fixed sun), which is why it never showed. |
| 2 | The fix | `render/toonSun.ts` replaces the toon material's lighting model once, for every `MeshToonMaterial` (it overrides `MeshToonNodeMaterial.prototype.setupLightingModel`; `ToonLightingModel` is not exported): the same 0.7 / 1.0 steps, but the light the step multiplies fades from the **unshadowed** light (`lightNode.baseColorNode`, before `ShadowNode` multiplied it) to the shadowed one over the first 0.08 of N·L. A back face is flat 0.7 and takes no shadow (the shadow lookup is still emitted, its result is multiplied by 0); a facing face is shadowed as before. Plus `shadow.normalBias` on both suns, `4 × half / 2048` (two texels of the orthographic frustum: about 0.024 in the office, scaled with the city's), the constant `bias` kept. Back faces stay flat rather than grey next to a lit wall: the whiteboard and the wall around it agree. A partition seen from behind is now the flat 0.7 tone (it used to be shadowed dark, striped at the edges). |
| 3 | Left as it was | The floor's shadows of desks, workers and mullions (they receive on a facing face; the edge moves a pixel or two with the normal bias); the shadow map size and the office frustum (no stripes remain at grazing angles with the bias). |
| 4 | Test | `e2e/m17-shadow-acne.spec.ts`: lat 51, 09:00 to 17:00 on 1 Oct, the issue's poses; `stripeScore` (`e2e/pixels.ts`, luma turning back on itself along the rows of a plain rectangle) over the whiteboard's margin, the partition, the sofa's back and the city's wall. The old code scores 10 to 150 where the new scores 0; the spec fails on it at 13:00, 15:00 and 17:00. |

## 2026-10-20 — #141: floor entry stops rebuilding every office shader

Every elevator ride, floor switch and "go to worker" on another floor froze the page for one frame of 650–1100 ms on
this host: the new `OfficeView` was all new instanced meshes and new lamps, and the old one was disposed before the
new one drew. three r186 keys an `InstancedMesh`'s compiled state by its uuid and captures its instance buffers when it
builds it (`RenderObject.js:846-850`, `nodes/accessors/Instance.js`), and every lit material's state by the lights' ids
(`LightsNode.js:147-169`), so the first frame built the whole office's node graphs and programs again. Code:
`game/src/render/floorPool.ts` (`FloorPool`, `PoolKit`, `poolCapacity`, `reuse`, `meshKit`), the kits in `crowd.ts`,
`desks.ts`, `tray.ts`, `cards/atlasCards.ts`, `monitorCards.ts`, `screens.ts`, `props.ts` (plants), `rack.ts` (LEDs),
`boards/noteBoard.ts`; `office.ts` (`officeLamps`, `holdsPool`, `dispose`); `app.ts` (`retire`, `render`,
`keepOutside`, `enterFloor`, `warmUp`), `render/disposeHold.ts`; the probe `floorPoolProbe.ts` (`__h3d.floorPool`); specs
`e2e/h141-floor-entry.spec.ts`, `e2e/h141-floor-entry-perf.spec.ts`, `src/render/floorPool.test.ts`.

| # | Question | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What is kept | **A pool of instanced meshes, one kit per part, outliving the offices** (`FloorPool`, owned by `App`). A kit is a part's instanced meshes with their geometry, material, per-instance attributes and atlas canvases (the crowd, the desks, the trays, the cards, the monitor cards, the frozen screens, the plants, the rack's LEDs, the two note boards). An office part takes its kit (`take(key, desks, holder, make)`) and writes its floor into it as a new mesh would be written: `count`, matrices, colours, attributes, a blank atlas. The office's other objects stay per office: their states are keyed by structure, not by object, and live on through the next office (row 3). |
| 2 | Why per uuid, and the rules | The key holds the uuid and the state holds that mesh's arrays, so a state is correct for one mesh only. **One live mesh per uuid; a kit's `instanceMatrix` / `instanceColor` attributes and arrays are never replaced; `instanceColor` exists from construction where a part uses colour; `count` never exceeds the capacity.** A pooled mesh, its material and geometry are never disposed by an office (`OfficeView.dispose`, `Crowd.dispose`, … skip them): only the pool disposes a kit, when it outgrows it. A part whose kit was taken by a newer office (`holder`) writes nothing to it, so the office you left cannot draw into the floor you are on. A uuid probe (the investigation's prototype: one uuid per mesh by position) was measured faster but binds one mesh's draws to another's buffers, and was not used. |
| 3 | Growth | Capacity in desks: `poolCapacity(need, had)`, a multiple of 16, and at least 1.5 × the kit it replaces, so a floor that outgrows a kit gets a new one (new meshes, new uuids, the old one disposed) a few times in a session at most. demo floors of 6–20 desks: 16 then 32; perf-floor60: 64. Fixed-size kits (plants 4, LEDs, notes) never grow. The capacity is baked into the shader (three's uniform path); the unused instances past `count` are not drawn. |
| 4 | The lamps | **The same two `SpotLight`s on every floor** (`FloorPool.lamps`), moved into each new office's root at the place they always had, their targets beside them; each office sets their position, target and intensity, and only the office they hang in can change them. Hidden and culled meshes of the office are shown and unculled for the moment the compile picks its objects (it does before its first `await`), so the crew's props and the trays come compiled too. `renderer.info.calls` is advanced first: three keeps the scene's cache key per render call (`NodeManager.getCacheKey`), and the lamps just came into the scene.
