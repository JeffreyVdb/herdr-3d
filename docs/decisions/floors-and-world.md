## 2026-09-30 — M17 (#50): day and night, the office clock

The game follows the viewer's local time (the browser's clock and zone), game only: no bridge or protocol change.
Decisions (orchestrator and user) and what was measured while building it:

| # | Question | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sunrise and sunset (open question) | **A latitude setting** (option b). With a latitude, the sun's altitude comes from the latitude, the solar declination of the day of the year and the hour angle, with **solar noon at 12:00 local standard time** (13:00 on the clock while the zone keeps summer time: the zone's offset against the smaller of its January and July offsets, from `Intl`). Daylight is a smoothstep of the altitude from −6° (civil twilight) to +7°, so the length of the day and of dawn follow the season and the latitude, and a polar day or night clamps. **No longitude** (a follow-up if ever): Brussels' sun sets about 45 min earlier than the real one. Without a latitude, the **fixed curve**: dawn 06:30–08:00, dusk 18:30–20:00 clock time, and a sun arc (up 07:15, down 19:15, 57° at 13:15) for the light's angle. Same output either way (`world/daylight.ts`, pure). |
| 2 | Where the zone and latitude are set | Client settings, in the **Settings** window since #91 (first in About, beside the limits switch): a timezone field (an IANA name, aliases such as `UTC` and `Europe/Kiev` accepted, validated by the `Intl.DateTimeFormat` constructor; numeric offsets refused: the setting asks for a place, whose summer time then applies; empty: the browser's zone, shown as "In effect") and a latitude field (−90…90, empty: the fixed curve). Commit on Enter or leaving the field, applied live; invalid input is refused under the field and not stored (`ui/timePrefs.ts`, `h3d.time.zone` / `h3d.time.lat`, every storage access guarded). `?tz=` and `?lat=` override them for a page load. |
| 3 | Pinning the hour | `?time=HH:MM` (today in the effective zone) and `?time=YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM`; a pinned time is frozen (the clock shows it, the light does not move) and starts there, no easing. Every e2e spec opens at `2026-09-30T12:00` unless it pins its own (`e2e/game.ts`), `make shot` included; the real-clock path is tested with `page.clock.setFixedTime` and `timezoneId`. `__h3d.daylight()` and `__h3d.clock()` let a test read the light and the clock without OCR. |
| 4 | Cadence | Read just past every minute (the clock turns on time), at once when a setting changes or the tab comes back; eased over about 3 s (exponential, landing on the target so a settled sky costs nothing). First load and a pinned time start at the target. |
| 5 | The baked street (open question) | **Runtime tint** of the baked street and facade by their material's colour, never a re-bake and no night bake. What glows is kept out of the tint: the lamp globes and the neighbours' lit windows are a baked mesh of their own, the lobby's room another (its own lights keep it bright: a milder warm tint), the fire-escape doors multiply from their day colour. The panes have their own node material, one uniform: by day their painted state colours as before; at night a running floor's panes glow warm and a sleeping floor's go nearly black, so the building shows which floors are alive. The street lamps come on with additive pools of light under them (one mesh, its opacity). |
| 6 | Office lights (open question) | A **constant light set**: adding, removing or hiding a light (or toggling `castShadow`) changes the lights' cache key and rebuilds render objects; intensity and colour, 0 included, are uniforms. The office surfaces are toon materials, which a RectAreaLight does not light, so the lamps are ****two** non-shadow spot lights** under the ceiling shining down on the desk area (a point light that high lit the 6.5 m ceiling more than the desks), full at night, 30 % by day, off on an asleep floor; no light per monitor, no new shadow caster. The ceiling panels, monitors and screens were already unlit colour and glow as before. **Measured** (`e2e/m17-perf.spec.ts`, perf-floor60: 60 workers, shadows on, WebGL2 on llvmpipe, 1280×720, looking across the desks and out of the windows; median serialized frame of 3 day/night pairs, two batches): origin/main 121.6 / 122.8 ms (its noon both times); **two lamps** noon 132.2 / 129.0 ms, 23:00 131.8 / 128.6 ms; three lamps noon 133.1 / 133.0 ms, 23:00 134.0 / 131.4 ms. Night costs what day costs (the same light set, the same 111 draws; origin/main 105: the lamps' glow mesh, the lobby's room, the pools and the clock). M17 as a whole is about +7 ms (+6 %) on the fallback; a third lamp adds about 3 ms more for a look two already give, so it stays at two. |
| 7 | Asleep and the time of day | They compose, neither setter overwrites the other (`WalkView.apply` reads both; the office's lamps read both): an asleep floor is darker than a running one at every hour (vitest over 24 hours, and the canvas luma in e2e: noon 190 vs 80, night 87 vs 21, running vs asleep). Day + asleep is the old asleep look; night + asleep nearly black. Noon awake is a little brighter than before (hemisphere 1.6, sun 2.1, from 1.5 and 1.9), the sun's noon direction unchanged in both scenes; the morning and evening sun swing around it. |
| 8 | The clock | A prop in `world/layout.ts` on the **board partition's north face** (the desks' side, 1.78–2.3 m, above the monitors, in view from every desk and from the mezzanine), on every floor. A digital face on a canvas (like the radio's display): HH:MM big, the date (`Wed 30 Sep`, English names whatever the locale) and a small sun or moon, in the effective zone, 24-hour, drawn only when that changes; backlit (basic material), so it reads at night. No analogue face. |
| 9 | Look-only props | `Prop.milestone: null` means the prop is only looked at: the bar shows its label and a line it is given (`Hud.setPropInfo(id, text)`; the clock's `14:05 · Wed 30 Sep · Europe/Brussels`) and no key, instead of "Placeholder until Mx". Not a special case of the clock's id. |
| 10 | Out of scope | Seasons beyond the latitude model, longitude, the receptionist's greeting by time of day, night-time crowd behaviour. |

## 2026-10-02 — #98: the desk monitor's activity card (game side)

- **One draw for every card on a floor.** The card (mascot, word, detail, counter) is one quad per desk in one

- **A label that disagrees with the status is not shown** (only `waiting` while idle or done), so a status event that
  beats the bridge's clearing event never leaves a finished turn's last word on the monitor; an unknown kind is Working.
- **The HUD's waiting count shows only while someone waits**: most of the time it is zero, and a fourth always-on count

## 2026-10-16 — #74: the server rack and the host's stats

A short server rack on every floor, between the lounge window and the whiteboard, with a monitor on it that shows the
bridge host's CPU, memory and (on Arch) pending updates, and LEDs that follow its disks. Protocol: "Host stats".
Code: `internal/hoststats`, `internal/server/hoststats.go`, `game/src/render/rack.ts`, `game/src/rack/link.ts`.

| # | Question | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sampling only while looked at | **One `hoststats.Sampler` per hub**, started by the first connection that sends `host.watch` and stopped by the last `host.unwatch` or disconnect. Between those it has no goroutine, no timer and no open file; `Stop` waits for the goroutine and a frame handed to the hub gives up with its run's context, so stopping never waits on the hub. A frame of an earlier run is dropped by a generation number. The limits poller (every client, broadcast to all) was not the model; `board.watch`'s per-connection counting was. |
| 2 | Read `/proc`, no gopsutil | A sample is ~30 µs; the cost is in watching, not in reading. gopsutil computes the same numbers (`Used = Total − Available`, guest time left out), keeps one program-wide CPU baseline the sampler would have to bypass, is cross-platform for a Linux-only bridge and adds 1 direct and 8 indirect modules. Half the parsing was in `server/load.go`, which now reads through `hoststats` (hire capacity unchanged; `hoststats` imports nothing of the module but `execpath`). Files are read into a reused buffer. |
| 3 | 5 s and 1 s | `host.stats` every 5 s (first one after 1 s from a baseline taken at the start); `host.disk` every 1 s as a separate small frame, because a 5 s average would smear a burst and the LEDs would feel late. The stats ticker starts with the sampler, so the second frame comes 4 s after the first. Nothing is queued per watcher: a slow client is dropped as for every other frame. |
| 4 | Updates | `checkupdates` only: it syncs a throwaway database, needs no root and cannot cause a partial upgrade; the bridge never runs `pacman -Sy`, anything as root or an upgrade. Supported = Arch (`ID` or `ID_LIKE`) **and** both `checkupdates` and `fakeroot` resolve (`execpath.Resolve`, and fakeroot's directory goes on `checkupdates`' `PATH`); asked again at every start. `CHECKUPDATES_DB` is a `checkupdates/` directory next to `world.db` (the script links `local` and creates `sync/` inside it and removes `db.lck` on exit, so not the state directory itself). **At most one attempt every 12 h, successful or not:** a failure keeps the last good count and its time and waits the full 12 h rather than hit the mirrors again from a bridge that is offline. The due check is noticed on the 5 s frame (an in-memory comparison) and never runs more than that. A run cancelled with the last watcher still counts as an attempt (review round 2: restoring the old time let watch, unwatch, watch cycles start a mirror sync each), and leaves the last good count alone.
