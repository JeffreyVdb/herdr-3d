## 2026-10-03 — #116: a new hire takes you to its transcript

| # | Question | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The setting | Settings → Display → "Go to a new hire after hiring" (`HirePrefs.goToNew`, `ui/hirePrefs.ts`, key `h3d.hire.goto`, browser-stored like the other display prefs). **On by default**; only "off" is stored. Off is today's behaviour exactly: the dialog closes, the toast, you stay where you are. |
| 2 | When it fires | After `worker.hire` is acknowledged **and the hire dialog is still the one that sent it** (`still` in the ack handler, `ui/hire.ts`): `HireHost.goTo(ack.id)`, wired in `app.ts` to `goToNewHire`, which ends in `goToWorker(id, 'transcript')` (the inbox's go-to: faces the worker, sits at its laptop, its conversation on screen). Also for `prompted: false` (the error toast stays; you land where you can prompt it with P). A hire with a first task lands on the transcript with that task as its first prompt, one without on an empty transcript. |
| 3 | Not on late acks | If you pressed Esc, moved on or opened another dialog (`still` false), the ack only toasts, as before: a late ack never yanks you. The same while it waits (4): the moment you act it is gone **for good**, not just while the state looks wrong at a poll: a key, a press or the wheel (`onPlayerAct`, `ui/hireGoto.ts`), any dialog taking `open` (so a Settings or About that opened and closed again between two polls counts), or a walker pose that is no longer the one you had when the ack came (moved, turned, sat). Besides, at every look and at fire time, a dialog of the game, **any open modal `<dialog>`** (Settings and About are not in `dialogOpen`, yet `dismissOverlays` would close them and seat you), the elevator or another panel, a seat you took, an elevator ride or leaving the floor drops it. Nothing is queued. |
| 4 | Waits for the upsert and the arrival | `goToNewHire` polls every 100 ms for at most **6 s** (`HIRE_GOTO_WAIT_MS`, `ui/hireGoto.ts`) until the worker is in the store (`worker.upsert`) **and** its drop-in has finished (`!arrivals.has(id)`, desk not in `awayDesks()`), then calls `goToWorker`; on timeout the toast stands alone. The deadline and your acting are checked before readiness at every look (`waitForHire`, `ui/hireGoto.ts`), so a timer that runs late (throttled or resumed tab) never acts on a readiness that came after the 6 s. *Ack before upsert?* On the bridge the ack follows the upsert (`runHire` waits for `op.seen`, set in the same pass that builds the upsert, and the stream is ordered), but the protocol does not promise it and the dialog already copes with a missing `workerView` (`w` null: the name from the form), so the game does not assume it. *`sitAt` during the arrival?* It does not fail for the drop-in itself (`whyNot` only refuses a missing worker, an `awayDesks()` desk, a corner desk, no pane, a sleeping floor; probed on the simulator: it sat 0.2 s into the drop), but the seat's camera would land on the chair while the figure still falls into it, so the go-to waits for the end of the arrival. Only a worker not yet in the store, or its desk still held `away` (the hire's own reservation, before the world shows it), makes `sitAt` toast "is not at the desk right now". |
| 5 | A go-to that cannot sit | `goToWorker` returning false, or leaving you facing the worker (asleep floor, no pane yet), is not an error: nothing is toasted by the hook and the toast already says "Here they come." (`sitAt`'s own reason shows as for any go-to). |
| 6 | Not for orchestrated runs | The start-run dialog does its own `followOrch` on start (you are turned to face its desk); it has no `goTo`, so there is no second jump. Its "hire at desk" tab opens the hire dialog, which does go. |
| 7 | Where it is not | Hiring for another floor from where you stand is not supported (`openHire` uses `hereSession()`), so the new worker is always on your floor; N, the inbox and E are unchanged. The #54 rule (a hire never moves you off your spot) still holds with the setting off, and with it on the move is the explicit go-to. |

## 2026-10-09 — #212 (phase 3 of #209): the accordion review of a dictated hire

A dictated hire always opens a review (`ui/hireReview.ts`, `HireReviewDialog`); the human's Hire is the only thing that
launches. Phase 4 (the dictation bar) opens it with `App.openHireReview(interpretation, desk?)`; until then it is built
and tested against fixture interpretations (`world/hireReviewFixtures.ts`, `window.__h3d.openHireReview`).

| # | Question | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A second launcher? | No. The review reuses the hire dialog's pieces (exported from `ui/hire.ts`: the chip and model pickers with their readable names (#200) and Settings' hidden models (#194), the worktree control, the capacity block), `hire.prepare` for folder, label, worktree and caps, `hireArgs` / `hireBlocker`, the desk rules (`resolveHireDesk`, which `App.openHire` now uses too) and **`HireCoordinator`**: the desk's hold, background mode, outcomes and the once-only key (`review:<dialog>:<epoch>`). |
| 2 | Fallbacks | **None.** `resolveChoice` / `resolveVisibleChoice` replace an unknown model with the first and a missing effort with `high`; the review never calls them (`world/hireReview.ts` has its own pure functions). A value that is missing or not valid is empty and its section is open; a harness change empties the model; a model change keeps a still-supported effort and clears any other (its word kept for the question). The bridge's floor default (`hire.prepare`'s `choice`) is never an agent choice. |
| 3 | Sections | Harness and model, Effort, Task, Location and other hire options. A valid section starts collapsed with its value in the header; a section that needs a choice starts open and stays open until the human closes it (several can be open; they are independent buttons with `aria-expanded` / `aria-controls`). Each says where its value came from: **Dictated**, **Resolved by the interpreter**, **Default** (the floor, name or folder the bridge offers), **Your choice** after an edit, **Not given** / **Needs a new choice** for a missing or invalid one. |
| 4 | Effort | Required and never preselected: the open section asks the interpreter's question and lists the model's levels. `answerEffort(level)` takes a dictated reply like a click (marked Dictated; a level the model does not support is refused and changes nothing). |
| 5 | Task | The complete text in an editable box; the header shows a one-line preview. Sent as the first task exactly as it stands (no images: the dictated hire has none). Empty, starting with `--`, or over the prompt cap blocks Hire. |
| 6 | Location | The floor and desk (the first free one, or the one given; a floor that is asleep or changing, a desk someone sits at or another hire holds, is refused with the dialog's own toasts), label, folder, worktree and the caps, all visible. A desk taken meanwhile blocks Hire and offers the next free one; the hard cap blocks it. |
| 7 | Late answers | A `ticket` (`begin()`: the review's epoch and its edit count) goes with a request to the interpreter; `apply(interpretation, ticket)` changes only the fields the human has not edited since, keeps the harness and model together, and is dropped when the review was cancelled or replaced. `hire.prepare` answers are guarded by their own counter and the epoch. A catalog that loses a model clears it (`refresh()`), never switches it. |
| 8 | Nothing launches early | No `<form>`: Enter in a field does nothing, toggles, picks, follow-ups and Cancel send nothing. Hire is a disabled button while a value is missing, with the reason beside it. The focus on opening is the first section that needs a choice, never Hire (the Enter that interpreted must not press it). |
| 9 | On Hire | The reviewed values are snapshotted into `HireRequest` and handed to the coordinator; the state goes to `hiring`, so a second press is ignored (and the coordinator would return the same operation). Background: the review closes at once and the coordinator toasts and beeps. Foreground: success closes it, goes to the worker and toasts as the hire dialog does; a refusal leaves the review as it was. An unknown outcome (timeout, dropped connection) is never retried by itself. |

## 2026-10-09 — #211 (phase 2 of #209): dictated hire requests become proposals, never hires

The bridge reads one dictated sentence into a hire proposal for the review dialog (phase 3), or into `unsupported` /
`unclear`. The research (`docs/research/dictation-hiring.md`) measured the configured LLM plus catalog validation at 50
of 50; this applies it. Code: `internal/hireintent`, `internal/server/hireintent.go`, `internal/proto/hireintent.go`;
protocol: [Hire interpretation](../protocol.md#hire-interpretation).

| # | Question | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Shape | `internal/hireintent` takes only `ai`'s LLM interface (`TestHireIntentUsesOnlyAIInterfaces`): `Interpret` (one call, `Parse`, `Resolve`) and `Run` (the role's readiness, the bound). No Jev gate (the research measured it rejecting valid examples), no switch or `*.test` of its own: nothing happens until the game sends a sentence, and an LLM that is not `ready` is a `not_ready` result. Only hiring is understood; `intent` leaves room for other explicit intents, none implemented. #190: the classifier's interface too, for near misses only (see #190 row 6). |
| 2 | The prompt and the LLM's schema | **The research's revised prompt, verbatim** (`prompt.txt` embedded), and its seven keys unchanged, so every recorded live answer replays as is and the dry-run check is the research's setup. The request is the research harness's: JSON mode, 1,400 tokens, no partial answer, no reasoning effort of its own, `{"command", "pending_hire"?, "hire_options"}` (#190: 3,000 tokens and low reasoning when the model takes it, `hire_options` without the hidden models; prompt and keys unchanged: see #190 rows 9, 10). The parse is strict (exactly those keys, enums for `intent` and `harness`); `model` and `effort` are free text so an unknown one becomes a question, not an error. |
| 3 | Dictated vs resolved | The LLM answers an exact catalog id when it resolves a name, so the spoken words of a valid model are not recoverable without changing the schema (which would void the measurement). Provenance says it instead: `dictated` when the value itself is said as a word, `resolved` when the catalog supplied it for what was said (a label, an alias, a harness from its model), `invalid` keeps the unrecognised words in `said`. |
| 4 | Validation | Every value against the bridge's **current** catalog, on every call: exact ids and readable labels (any case), the harness named or, with none, exactly one harness; a model of the other harness, unknown or contradictory is `invalid` and asked about with the catalog's choices, never substituted. No defaults from floor settings, the last hire, `hire.prepare` or the game's `resolveChoice`. The tuple rule is one: `hireintent.Catalog.Check`, which the bridge's `validHireIn` (worker.hire, settings, presets) now calls; `proto` cannot be imported there (it imports the feature's types), so the server converts `proto.HireOptions` field for field. #190: the visible models only (`hidden_models`), and families, versions and near misses resolve too (see #190 rows 1-6); `Check` keeps the whole catalog. |
| 5 | Effort | Required, never defaulted: missing asks exactly "What effort level should this agent use?" with the model's levels (common ∪ extra). On top of the prompt's rule, an effort counts only when its word (or "extra high", "maximum") is said **outside the task span**: a task word the LLM mistakes for the effort is dropped, and asking is the safe side. #190: the LLM's answer is canonicalized first ("X high" → `xhigh`, see #190 row 4). |
| 6 | The task | A **source span** in UTF-16 code units (packup's units) into the sentences, found deterministically: the latest sentence holding the LLM's task text, its last occurrence there (a hire wrapper comes before its task). Not an exact part of what was said: `bad_span`, no proposal. |
| 7 | Follow-ups without state | The bridge keeps nothing. The client sends the pending proposal back; its sources go to the LLM as `pending_hire` (the research's history), its task span and field shapes are checked (`bad_pending`; a valid harness must be `claude` or `codex`), its values resolved again against the current catalog: a harness with no models there is `invalid` (said kept, not carried to the next reply) and the model question offers every model of the catalog, never an empty list nor another harness's model in its place. A field the LLM leaves out keeps the pending value; the task changes only to a part of the reply; a model change drops an effort the new model lacks to `missing`. At most 6 sentences. #190: and against the `hidden_models` of that call (see #190 row 2). |
| 8 | Ready means review | `ready_for_review` = every field valid and nothing to ask. Nothing in the result launches; phases 3 and 4 open the review and wait for the human's confirmation. |
| 9 | Failures | Operational errors are the result's `error {code, message}` (the `ai.Kind`, `bad_answer`, `bad_span`, `bad_pending`), the text kept, no proposal; the command fails only on bad args. Messages are fixed text (a provider's may echo the sentence). Bounded at 35 s on the bridge: the AI integration's two 15 s attempts. |
| 10 | Logs and audit | Like `packup.detect`: never a sentence. The log has the intent, readiness, question count and error code; the audit the text's length and the pending sources' count. |
| 11 | Evidence | `go test ./internal/hireintent` replays the 50 bench cases with their recorded live answers (`testdata/bench.json`, the live catalog recorded with them), asserting outcome, exact task, span and every field's provenance; `docs/research/dictation-hiring-bench/dryrun` replays them live through the package, the original texts against the live catalog (a dry-run interpretation check, not a launch test; its result is recorded in the pull request, not committed). The unit-test fixture holds only the selected models (four Claude models and `gpt-6.1-sol`): its four GPT-6-Sol cases name GPT-6.1-Sol, their recorded answers `gpt-6.1-sol`. |

| # | Question | Decision |

| 4 | Keys in the field | Enter sends (Ctrl/⌘+Enter too, the dialogs' submit); Shift+Enter is a new line; a pasted text keeps its line breaks. An IME's Enter never sends: `isComposing`, `keyCode === 229` (Safari's committing Enter after `compositionend`) and a beat after `compositionend`. Paste and printable keys are never prevented.
