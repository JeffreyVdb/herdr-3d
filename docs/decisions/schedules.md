# Schedules decisions

## 2026-10-16 — #62 (part 1 of #61): the bridge's schedule store and runner

Agents end turns with work that can only happen later ("24h soak, until about 10:45 UTC tomorrow; then delete the
backups"). The bridge now stores one-shot schedules and, when one is due, hires a fresh worker with its prompt, with
no client connected (protocol: "Scheduled agents"). Code: `internal/store/schedule.go`, `internal/server/schedule.go`,
`internal/proto/schedule.go`, `handoff.HireTask`, `world.Listed`. The game's dialog and boards are parts 3 and 4.

| # | Question | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | One code path for a hire | **The fire is `worker.hire`'s hire**: `beginHire` / `runHire` / `finishHire`, unchanged in what they do. A schedule passes the bridge's own `Client` identity (`bridge:schedule`: the audit log's client; never connected, so its replies go nowhere) and a `hireWatch`: `begun` writes `running` with the minted worker id before anything reaches herdr (an error refuses the hire), `settled` ends the schedule from `finishHire`. No copy of the hire's checks, caps or rollback. Clients see a bridge hire exactly like a game's: its `floor.upsert`, `worker.upsert`, statuses. |
| 2 | The clock | **The wall clock, looked at**: every 30 s, once on startup when discovery's first `world.Listed` says the floors are known, and when a floor is first seen. Never a `time.Timer` to the due time: a monotonic timer stops while the host is suspended. `Options.Now` is the clock, so every test runs on an injected one. |
| 3 | Missed window | A schedule first **seen** due within `due_at + 5 min` fires normally (two missed 30 s looks and a slow restart still fit). Past it: `skip` → `skipped` "missed: bridge was offline at <due> (seen <now>)"; `run_late` (default) over `max_lateness` → `skipped` naming the lateness; else it fires with the late notice first in its prompt ("This task was scheduled for <due UTC> and is running at <now UTC>, <dur> late. Check whether anything below is stale before acting."). `seen_at` keeps when it was first seen, so a schedule that then waits (a waking floor) is not judged again. Run now and retry skip the policy; a retry past the grace still gets the notice. |
| 4 | Once, across restarts | `running` is written before herdr is called. On startup a `running` schedule is `done` if the bridge ever recorded its worker (the `worker` table, closed or not), else `failed` "bridge stopped while hiring". Never fired twice. |
| 5 | When the floor cannot take it | Floor gone (discovery no longer lists its session, or a deleted virtual floor) → `failed`. Asleep: `wake_floor` (default on) wakes it through `wakeFor`, the path a hire on an asleep virtual floor already took (#58), audited as the bridge's `floor.wake`; the schedule is `waiting` and fires when the wake ends; a failed wake fails it. Off → `failed` "floor asleep: wake_floor is off". Waking or sleeping (`busy`), not seen yet, or no free desk → `waiting`, tried on the next look. A floor is "gone" only once discovery has listed the sessions: before that the bridge does not know. |
| 6 | Desk, cwd, caps | The bridge picks the **first free desk** (no worker holds it, no hire on its way) within the floor's desks; the hire grows the floor as any hire does. `cwd` missing → `failed` "working directory <cwd> no longer exists", never another directory. A hard cap → `failed` with the hire's message, which names `--max-workers-per-floor` / `--max-workers`. |
| 7 | The prompt and its handoff | The schedule's prompt, then the handoff line in the cabinet's Hire from handoff wording, from one Go helper (`handoff.HireTask`; `TestHireTaskIsTheGames` reads `game/src/world/handoff.ts` and checks the template is the same text). A deleted handoff: "The handoff <path> this task referenced was deleted; continue without it." |
| 8 | Outcomes | A hire that succeeds → `done` (`worker_id`, `fired_at`). A hire error → `failed` with the hire's message. A worker hired but whose prompt was not sent (herdr refused it, Codex's hooks review) → **`failed`**, naming the worker, who stays: the task did not reach anyone, so it needs attention; a retry hires a new worker. `failed` and `skipped` stay in view until dismissed. |
| 9 | Never destructive | No schedule path sends a worker home or deletes a worktree, branch or workspace; every fire test checks herdr's and git's calls and the audit log for it. The one rollback a fire can cause is `worker.hire`'s own: a hire that fails after herdr made its workspace closes that fresh workspace (a fresh worktree only if clean and its branch still at its base), as any failed hire does. |
| 10 | Store and view | SQLite `schedule`, one row per schedule, every transition written first (`updated_at`). The view (snapshot `schedules`, `schedule.upsert` / `schedule.removed`) is the upcoming, the failed and skipped not dismissed, and each floor's last 50 `done`; older `done` rows are deleted. Cancelled and dismissed rows are kept, out of view; a command on one is `invalid_state`. A schedule's floor and source cannot change; `update` replaces the rest of its spec. |

## 2026-10-16 — #63 (part 2 of #61): a schedule holds its worktree

A schedule stores its `cwd`. When that is a linked worktree, sending its worker home with "delete worktree" or a
wrap-up armed with a delete would remove the checkout, and the schedule would fail hours later. Now a schedule that may
still fire holds the worktree, and nothing in the bridge deletes it (protocol: "Scheduled agents", "Worktree hold").
Code: `internal/server/schedulehold.go`, the checks in `startFirePrepare`, `newFireOp`, `fireCheck`, `runFire`,
`startWrapUp` and `wrapUpClean`, and the removal's reservation (`reserveRemoval`; `removingRefusal` in
`scheduleCommand`).

| # | Question | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What holds | A schedule in `scheduled` or `waiting` whose `cwd` is the worktree's top or under it, **whatever its `worktree` flag**: a worktree hire still finds its repository from `cwd`. `running`, `done`, `failed`, `skipped`, `cancelled` and dismissed ones hold nothing. |
| 2 | Comparing paths | Both sides cleaned and, where they exist, symlinks resolved (`filepath.EvalSymlinks`, as repository discovery does); then equal, or a prefix ending on a separator (`/x/wt` holds `/x/wt/sub`, not `/x/wt2`). The worktree's side is the checkout (`worktree.checkout_path`, else the worker's cwd) when the command arrives, and git's top level (`readTree`) wherever git is read. |
| 3 | No cached hold | The hold is derived from the schedules each time it is asked (`Hub.scheduleHolds`, on the hub goroutine, which owns them; a copy is then matched off it, so path resolution never runs under the hub while git does). Cancel, fire and dismiss release it with nothing to clear. |
| 4 | The reason | `used by scheduled agent '<title>' at <due>`; several holders: the earliest due is named, then ` and <n> more`. One string for `worker.fire.prepare`'s `reason` and the `invalid_state` messages (`invalid state: <reason>`). It comes before a dirty or unpushed reason: commit and push would not make the delete possible. |
| 5 | "Local" on the bridge | The zone of the bridge's clock (`Options.Now`; `time.Local`, from `TZ` or the host, in production), `2006-01-02 15:04 MST`. A schedule's own reasons and prompts stay UTC (#62): they go to an agent and must not depend on the host. The hold's reason is for the person at the send-home dialog, on the same host. Tests inject a clock in a fixed zone. |
| 6 | When it is checked | At command time, not only in the dialog: `newFireOp` (worker.fire, worker.wrapup's arm) refuses a delete against the checkout, and a CLEAN looks before it and goes home with `keep`; `fireCheck` (before anything is touched, and again after the agent exited) matches git's top, so a worker whose cwd is under its worktree is held by a schedule at the top. |
| 7 | A wrap-up armed before the schedule | Its CLEAN **falls back to keep**: the worker goes home, the workspace closes, worktree and branch stay; the note `<reason>: the worktree is kept` is in the `worker.fire` audit row (`wrapup.reason`, and a step). Not `needs_you`: the agent reported clean, and keep is what the person would choose now. Never a delete. |
| 8 | A hold during the agent's exit | `worker.fire` keeps the worktree with a `note`, as it does for a tree that changed then; a wrap-up's send home does the same rather than its strict failure, since keeping is safe. |
| 9 | Outside the bridge | `git worktree remove` or `rm` are not prevented; the schedule fails when it fires (#62: `working directory <cwd> no longer exists`). |
| 10 | The game | The send-home dialog already showed `reason` ("Deleting is off: <reason>. …") and offers only keep. It recognises a hold by the reason's prefix (`scheduleHold`, `world/hire.ts`; the prefix is part of the protocol): the callout then says the worktree stays until that agent starts or its schedule is cancelled, and "commit and push first" is said only when something is left on the desk (dirty or unpushed). A `worker.fire` / `worker.wrapup` refused `invalid_state` for a hold (a schedule made after the dialog looked) shows the bridge's reason, not the asleep-floor text the other `invalid_state`s of `worker.fire` get, and the dialog looks again and falls back to keep, as after `not_clean`. |
| 11 | No schedule between the last check and the removal (review R1) | A hold read on the hub and then matched while git runs leaves a gap: a `schedule.create` acknowledged during the last git checks would still see its worktree removed. So the last check runs **on the hub, in the step that reserves the worktree** (`Hub.reserveRemoval`, right before `herdr worktree remove`): a schedule acknowledged before that step is seen and the worktree kept (`worker.fire`: a `note`, as #8; a wrap-up's CLEAN: keep, as #7). Until the removal ends, removed or failed (`releaseRemoval`, before the result goes out), `schedule.create`, `schedule.update` and `schedule.retry` (a retry that waits holds again) whose `cwd` is in that top answer **`busy`**, not `invalid_state`: nothing is wrong with the schedule or its state, the conflict passes within seconds, and once the worktree is gone the `cwd` check says why. `run_now` and `cancel` act on a schedule that already holds or releases. The reservation is in memory only: a restart ends every send home in flight (none resumes), and an armed wrap-up that resumes after one checks again at its CLEAN. |

The game shows and makes #62's scheduled agents: a HUD dialog with the floor's schedules in three sections and every
Part 1 action, one create / edit form, "Schedule follow-up…" beside a reply's Handoff button, and toasts when a schedule

follow-up button in `laptop/transcriptRender.ts`, `App.openSchedules` / `openFollowup` / `scheduleToasts`; the fixture
bridge's `tools/fixture-schedule.ts`. Tests: `scheduleTime.test.ts` (Temporal as the oracle), `schedule.test.ts`,
`schedules.dom.test.ts`, `scheduleForm.dom.test.ts`, `hudToast.dom.test.ts`, `transcriptHandoff.dom.test.ts`,
`tools/fixture-schedule.test.ts`, `e2e/schedules.spec.ts`; the dialog and the form are surfaces of `e2e/ui-surfaces.ts`.

| # | Question | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Where `running` goes | **Upcoming**, at its due time, with a "Starting" tape and no actions: it is not done yet, and Run now, Edit and Cancel all answer `invalid_state` for it. It moves to Scheduled successfully (or Needs attention) with the upsert that ends the fire. No toast for `running`: the toast is for the outcome. |
| 2 | What "fired" means | A live upsert into `done` (the hire went through) is "started"; into `failed` or `skipped` says so with its `state_reason`. Each toast has the title and a **Show** button. |
| 3 | Late | `fired_at − due_at` over the bridge's 5-minute grace (`ScheduleGrace`) is late: the toast says "started 3h late" and the row a "+3h late" badge (`spanText`: `7m`, `2h 20m`, `1d 4h`). Never for a negative value (Run now before its time) or within the grace, whatever the policy. |
| 4 | Wall clock to UTC | **Temporal's `disambiguation: 'compatible'`**, without Temporal (stable Safari lacks it): `wallToEpochMs` takes the zone's offsets a day either side; one candidate that reads back wins, two (a fall-back overlap) give the **earlier** instant, none (a spring-forward gap) uses the offset before the change, so the time moves later by the gap's length (30 min on Lord Howe, not a fixed hour). `daylight.zonedInstant` (a gap one hour later, an overlap's second showing) is the scene clock's and is unchanged. The resolver matched Temporal on 1.26M quarter hours (12 zones, 2025–2027) in the research; the tests compare it with Node 26's Temporal on every quarter hour of the 2026 transition days of Brussels, New York and Lord Howe, and every 7 h of 2026 in five zones. The oracle is typed locally in the test, so no `esnext.temporal` lib reaches the app's program. A "tomorrow" oracle must convert a `PlainDateTime`: `ZonedDateTime.with` keeps today's offset in an overlap (`offset: 'prefer'`) and gives the later instant. |
| 5 | The grammar | Relative: `in` + durations of `d` / `h` / `m` (and their words), summed: `in 24h`, `in 90m`, `in 1h30m`, `in 2 days`; elapsed time from now, so `in 24h` across a DST change is 24 real hours (and keeps now's milliseconds). Wall clock in the browser's zone (`browserZone()`, passed in: the parser reads no host clock or zone): `YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM` (also `T` between, seconds allowed: a `datetime-local` input's value), `today HH:MM`, `tomorrow HH:MM` (the zone's next calendar date, however long that day is), a bare `HH:MM` (today; once passed, the error says "tomorrow HH:MM"). A trailing `UTC` (or `Z`) reads both the date and the time in UTC (`tomorrow 10:45 UTC` is 10:45 on the next UTC day); with `in …` it is an error. Now or earlier is an error (the bridge refuses it). Relative input is read again at Save and every 15 s while the form is open, so the shown time stays true. |
| 6 | Showing a time | The row's stamp is the local `HH:MM` and date; beside it the UTC hint (`08:45 UTC`, with the date when UTC's differs) and a live countdown, which ticks once a second only while the dialog is open and is updated in place (no re-render). The form shows `Sun 18 Oct 2026, 10:45 CEST · 08:45 UTC · in 23 h 59 min` before Save is enabled. Names are English and the zone's short name is US English's, or British English's when US English only has `GMT+2`, so the text reads the same on every host. |
| 7 | The form's prefill | "New": `hire.prepare` for the floor, as the hire dialog (`formFromPrepare`: the floor's default or last hire, its folder, the worktree from Settings). A floor that cannot prepare (a real floor asleep: `hire.prepare` refuses it) falls back to the floor's settings (`default_hire`, `default_cwd`) with a note that the folder and worktree are checked when saved: a schedule for an asleep floor is what `wake_floor` is for. Edit: the schedule as it is (the floor fixed, its handoff kept unless changed, its `due_at` sent back to the millisecond while the When field still reads as it opened, the picker's echo of it included: read back, the minute-only wall clock would lose the seconds, and in a fall-back overlap the second showing would become the first, an hour early; the preview shows the kept instant with its own offset, `02:30 CET`). The follow-up: the source worker's harness, model, effort and cwd, `hire.prepare` with that cwd for the git base, an empty prompt, no time. Floor default to Save: `wake_floor` is always sent (its absence means on). |
| 8 | One op per reply | "Attach this message as a handoff" files a literal handoff on Save (`worker.handoff {mode: 'literal'}`), then `schedule.create` with its path. The form keeps **one op per reply** (worker, candidate, revision) for the page's life and, once the filing acks, its path: a refused create keeps the form open and a re-save does not file again; nor does the form opened again on the same reply after a Cancel. The path is reused rather than the op resent because the bridge checks the candidate before the op: after a new prompt the same op answers `handoff_unavailable`, not `existing`. The op is resent only when no answer came (a time-out, a drop), and then the bridge answers `existing`. A filing that acks after the form was cancelled or closed is still recorded on the reply. While a save is in flight, Floor and Folder are locked and the spec is the one Save read; a new open or a close ends it (its answer is dropped). A filing error schedules nothing and says why in the form; Cancel before Save files nothing. |
| 9 | Toasts | Detected in `onWorldEvent` before the store takes the upsert: only a schedule the store knows, whose state changed. A snapshot (first load, a reconnect) resets the book, so nothing in it is a transition; a re-delivered upsert has the same state. A transition that happened while the game was disconnected and comes in a `since_seq` replay toasts once, as `wrapUpToasts` does. No toast while the dialog is open: its row shows the change. |
| 10 | A toast's click | `Hud.toast(msg, ms, kind, soft, action?)`: with `{label, run}` the toast is a control (pointer events and a pointer only then, a **Show** button for the keyboard, the text in the live region as before); a click on it or its button runs the action and hides it. A plain toast, or one that timed out, takes no click and holds no button. Existing callers are unchanged. The schedule toasts open the dialog on the row (`openSchedules({focus})`: its floor, marked, focused). |
| 11 | Entry points | The Menu's Schedules (in "This floor" on a floor; in "City" elsewhere, on all floors) and a key-less palette row, "Scheduled agents". No keyboard shortcut (out of scope), so `docs/shortcuts.md` and `?` are unchanged. `openSchedules({floor?, allFloors?, focus?})` is the one way in (Part 4's boards will use it). |
| 12 | All floors | Off by default on a floor; outside a floor the dialog opens on all floors (the toggle is off there). All floors keeps the sections and groups each section's rows by `Building › floor` (`inboxWhere`), in the city's order; each floor's last 50 done are its own, so one busy floor never hides another's. |
| 13 | Errors | A refused command (`invalid_state`, `not_found`, `busy`, `bad_request`, no answer) is said in the dialog's or the form's alert line, with the bridge's reason and what to do, not only in a toast. An edited schedule that moves on or goes while its form is open says so in the form, and Save stays off. |

| 6 | Validation | Any of these is **no suggestion, recorded as seen**: `due_at` not RFC 3339 or without an offset / `Z`: `time.Parse(time.RFC3339)` refuses both, and the exact shape is checked too, because Go's parser also takes a one-digit hour, a `,` fraction and offsets like `+24:00` or `+01:60` (which shift the instant silently): two-digit fields, a `.` fraction of any length, an offset hour ≤ 23 and minute ≤ 59, upper-case `T` and `Z` only (Go refuses the lower-case ones RFC 3339 allows; the request asks for "an explicit offset or Z"); at or before now (the clock **when the answer came**, `TestValidatedAtAnswer`); more than 30 days after it; an empty title or prompt; a prompt over 32 KiB or starting with `--`; unparseable JSON; a cut-off answer (`ai.KindTruncated`); a timeout or any other failed call. `due_at` is kept in UTC. | 9 | Dedup and closing | Its own table, `schedsuggest_seen` (worker, SHA-256, the suggestion as JSON, `outcome`: `accepted`, `dismissed`, `moved`, `stale`, `gone`; the last 20 per worker), not a column on `followup_seen`: the two features look at a message independently (one may be off, or switched on later), and each must know only whether **it** looked.
