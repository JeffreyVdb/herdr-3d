## 2026-10-02 — #110: pack up on request

| # | Question | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Shape | `internal/packup` is `internal/followup`'s: `Run` (the classifier gate, then the LLM), `Service` (the settings row, the env override, readiness, the 30-minute test of the models in use, `ai.ErrNotReady` before any call when off or not ready), and it takes only `ai`'s interfaces (`TestPackupUsesOnlyAIInterfaces`). The hub's `packup.*` commands mirror `followups.*`; `packup.update` is its own event type (`proto.PackupUpdate`, as `activity.update` is), since its flat fields are `followups.update`'s. |
| 2 | The LLM's answer | **Spans to remove, never a rewrite.** Each must be an exact substring of what the models saw, found in order after the previous one; image stand-ins may not be cut. The bridge cuts them and tidies only whitespace and dangling separators at the joins. Anything invalid, any failure and the time bound (#12) are no match: the prompt goes as written. A failure never eats a prompt. |
| 3 | Offsets | `removed` is in UTF-16 code units, the game's string indexes, so the confirm strikes the right characters without re-encoding. The game still checks the ranges (sorted, apart, inside the text) and sends as written when they are not. |
| 4 | Images | The models see `[Image #n]` for each marker, never its path; a span over one is invalid. The marker stays in the task as it was. |
| 5 | Nothing left | `matched: true` with no task: the game opens the normal send-home dialog, nothing is sent. |
| 6 | Who | The wrap-up's workers: Claude or Codex agents, freelancers skipped (`world.Freelancer`), checked on the bridge and, to spare the round trip, in the game. |
| 7 | The hand-off | "Send and go home" is `worker.wrapup` with the task, a blank line, and the cleanup request ("Once that is done: " + the wrap-up's own prompt). No new teardown, no turn-end tracking. The #83 footer now reads "or, if the task did not complete, anything could not be stopped or needs a decision", so a failed task on a clean, pushed tree reports BLOCKED and the worker stays. A wrap-up armed by an older bridge with the old footer and resumed after the upgrade is no longer recognised and times out to needs you. |
| 8 | Threshold | **0.85 by default**, higher than follow-ups' 0.7: a false positive costs one dismiss, a false negative is today's behaviour. The gate question was calibrated against `jev-latest` and `jev-preview` in 1.20.1 (the first wording scored the ticket's "leave the office and clean up the worktree" at 0.3; the reworded one 0.93, 30 of 31 samples right at 0.75, skips at most 0.2); the threshold itself is untested beyond that: the labelled samples (31 prompts) are in `packup.Samples`, and `packup.test`'s `right` of `total` at a threshold is the calibration to run live. |
| 9 | The test | Only the four core samples gate a pass (each match cut to its exact task, the negative under the threshold); the rest are reported, so a model that misreads one paraphrase can still be switched on, and the count shows how good the threshold is. The samples are classified four at a time. #189: six positives (three core, three calibration) have expected remaining tasks and go to the LLM when gated. Gate-only rows are labelled explicitly; classifier calibration and verified extraction counts are separate. Each sample shares Detect’s total deadline and the AI call limiter; a late answer never passes. A passing test covers role fingerprints, threshold, reasoning effort, and completion budget; a failed retest revokes it. |
| 10 | Seated | The confirm (and the send home for a prompt with nothing left) keeps you at the laptop you sent from: any other dialog stands you up, which would drop the prompt box and its draft. The dialog still owns the keys. |
| 11 | Where it applies | The laptop's prompt box and the P overlay (both use the shared composer). A hire's first prompt is later (its worktree exists only after the hire); prompts typed into the terminal are already sent. |
| 12 | The 1.5 s bound | **The game holds a prompt at most 1.5 s from Send** (`packup.detect`'s timeout in `net/client.ts`): past it the prompt goes as written and a later ack, a match included, is dropped, so no confirm opens. The bridge bounds its models at **1.2 s** (`packup.DetectTimeout`), leaving its no match 0.3 s to reach the game; the model bound alone could not cover the hub's queue or a slow link. #189 keeps this deliberate send-latency policy: users may select higher effort, but Test reports incompatible latency without downgrading it. A configurable longer wait was considered and deferred; it would require coordinating the browser hold, bridge deadline, and a cancellation interaction. Increasing tokens or silently stretching the deadline is not a repair for exhaustion. |
| 13 | Logs | The bridge logs a detect's score, whether it matched and the task's length; a failure only as a fixed code (`ai.Kind`, or `llm_<kind>`, `bad_answer`, `bad_part` for an extraction): never an error's text, since a provider may quote the prompt in it. |

#189 adds saved **Reasoning effort** (Provider default plus the catalog's supported values) and **Completion budget**
(default 600, 64–16384) directly to Pack up on request. Explicit effort and budget override shared endpoint controls
for this feature alone. Unsupported saved efforts pause checks and require an explicit correction. `NoPartial` and
sanitized call diagnostics distinguish exhaustion from empty answers, schema/span errors, and timing failures.
JSON-object compatibility fallback remains; provider strict schema is deferred until capability detection exists,
and would still need local exact-span validation and exhaustion handling. Wrap-up and confirmation are unchanged.


## 2026-10-09 — #215: a report request can be resolved without resuming the worker, and is kept whole

A coder that ended its turn with `input_required` in its report stayed NEEDS YOU / "Waiting for an answer" after the orchestrator accepted the request in its ledger and moved on: nothing in the report changed. The request was also cut to 500 bytes, losing the decision itself.

| # | Question | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The resolution signal | A **sidecar file**, `<run>/<stem>.resolved.json` next to `<run>/<stem>.json` (`runs.ResolvedName`), `{"report", "resolved_by"?, "note"?}`, written atomically by the orchestrator. | 2 | Binding | The sidecar resolves the report only if its **mtime ≥ the report's**, compared at read time. | 4 | Tracking | The poller (`reportTracker`, off the hub goroutine) stats the report **and** its sidecar and re-reads when either stamp changed, so creating, replacing or deleting the sidecar is one `worker.upsert` within the poll interval. |
| 5 | Wire | `report.input_resolved {by, note?, at}`; `input_required` stays present (the card shows the note, the text is history). Also `input_truncated` and `input_for`. |
